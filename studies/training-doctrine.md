# Training doctrine

Tom's standing doctrine, first recorded 2026-09-03. It's the framework for training decisions going forward, not just for the current cohort. Moved here from the report's Training Strategy tab on 2026-09-24.

While Division III is an easy climb, the promotion-era priority overrides the order below: develop players cheaply and keep salaries low. See [Training strategy: promotion-era priorities](../references/training_strategy_notes.md).

## Big men (C / PF)

1. **ISP first.** Inside Shot, Inside Defense and Rebounding are the priority group.
2. **Shot Blocking deliberately lower** than the other three inside skills: it adds too much salary for the value it returns.
3. Passing and Driving are important: the next tier.
4. Shooting (Jump Shot) and Outside Defense come after that.
5. Jump Range is generally not a priority.

## Guards (PG / SG)

1. **OSP first:** the outside-skill group.
2. **Jump Range deliberately lower** than the other outside skills. It's the same salary-cost logic as Shot Blocking for bigs.
3. Passing is important, even more so for point guards.
4. Inside Scoring and Rebounding also matter for guards.

## Universal targets

For every position: **Stamina 6–8**, **Free Throw 7+**. Both have no salary effect at any rating (see [Skill salary costs](skill-salary-costs.md)).

## Team Training vs. individual skills

**Official · training.aspx mechanics.** Stamina and Free Throw can only be trained in **Team Training** mode (alongside Game Shape).

- Team Training can't run alongside the 10 individual position-skill categories: only one training focus is active for the whole team at a time.
- So reaching the Stamina and Free Throw targets means switching to Team Training from time to time. While it's active, individual skill development pauses for everyone.

## Where the report uses this

The Training Strategy tab's "Doctrine priority" recommendation mode checks each group's weakest priority skill against these lists.
