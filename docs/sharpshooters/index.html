What counts as an "investment" here

Every dated capital outlay pulled from the official economy ledger — player transfer purchases (and sales, if any) and lump-sum facility spend like the arena expansion — accumulated into a running ledger each time this report runs, since the API only ever shows the current and prior week. Recurring weekly costs (salaries, scouting) are operating expenses, not investments, but they do roll into each player's TCO (total cost of ownership: price paid + salary paid while owned) below, so cost-per-player is measured against the full bill, not just the transfer fee. Arena spend is tracked the same way against real gate revenue, bucketed automatically by capacity/price regime as arena.aspx changes.

Season structure (both analyses below assume this)

Official · game schedule mechanics  A season runs 14 weeks, but its two weekly resets don't both land 14 times: 13 Monday resets (TV money, merchandise, and player salaries are all paid on this reset — this is what a "season" of salary cost actually means below) and 14 Friday resets (training progress and weekly minutes tracking reset here instead). Any dollar figure below scaled per-season uses 13, not 14 — a season's salary bill is one payday short of what a flat 14-week count would suggest.

Bench-player acquisitions: which one is cheapest, long-run?

A total-cost-of-ownership comparison of three hypothetical bench-player acquisitions, with salary projected forward rather than held flat — using a decay curve fit from 15 real players' actual season-by-season salary histories.

The decay model

Player salaries peak around age 33–34, then decline. Fit from the 15-player sample (log-linear regression, R²=0.24 — larger salaries drop sharper): each season past the peak, % change ≈ 14.1% − 4.02%×ln(salary). A $200/week floor applies (no tracked player was ever observed below it). Age advances 1 year/season; each season's salary cost is that season's rate × 13 (Monday paydays, not a flat 14 — see "Season structure" above).

[Inference] The $200 floor is empirical, drawn from the sample's observed minimum — not a documented game rule, and could be wrong for a large enough roster.

Calculated Every skill except Stamina/Free Throw follows the same two-regime shape — a nearly flat "low regime" up to a per-skill breakpoint, then a much steeper "high regime" for the rest of the range (not a smooth power law). Baseline weekly salary with everything at rating 10 is $17,609 — every curve passes through that exact value, confirming the isolated-skill queries are internally consistent.

[Inference] Stamina and Free Throw training is essentially "free" salary-wise — push those to the 6–8 / 7+ targets already in this team's training doctrine without hesitation. Jump Shot and Rebounding are the two skills where a big push has the largest, most convex salary consequence — worth being deliberate about timing (e.g. concentrated pushes rather than a slow drip once past each skill's breakpoint). The separate 266-player market regression (pooled model, R²=0.891) found Age has no real effect on salary once current skills are controlled for — BuzzerBeater recalculates salary once per season from current skills only, so an apparent "age discount" on a young, still-training player is really just salary that hasn't been recalculated since their last training gains, not a market age premium.

General training priorities

Your stated doctrine
Standing training doctrine

Recorded here as the standing framework for training decisions going forward, not just for the current cohort.

Big men (C / PF)
  1. ISP first — Inside Shot, Inside Defense, Rebounding are the priority group.
  2. Shot Blocking deliberately lower than those other three ISP skills — it adds too much salary for the value it returns.
  3. Passing and Driving are important — next tier.
  4. Shooting (Jump Shot) and Outside Defense — after that.
  5. Jump Range — generally not a priority.
Guards (PG / SG)
  1. OSP first — the outside-skill group.
  2. Jump Range deliberately lower than the other outside skills — same salary-cost logic as Shot Blocking for bigs.
  3. Passing is important — even more so for point guards.
  4. Inside Scoring and Rebounding also matter for guards.
Universal training targets

Universal targets, regardless of position: Stamina 6–8, Free Throw 7+.

Team Training vs. individual skills

Official · training.aspx mechanics  Stamina and Free Throw are only trainable through Team Training mode (alongside Game Shape), which is mutually exclusive with the 10 individual position-skill categories — only one training focus is active for the whole team at a time. Hitting the Stamina 6–8 / Free Throw 7+ targets means periodically rotating into Team Training, which pauses individual skill development for everyone while it's active.

Training type efficiencyⓘReference Captured by Tom outside the game/API (references/Weekly Training Amounts.webp and training_coefficients.md in the GitHub repo) — not pulled live, so treat it as a training-mechanics reference rather than official documentation. Each row is the per-week skill-point gain rate for a training type + position combo, for a player who clears their weekly minutes threshold at that position. “Sum” adds every skill a combo touches into one efficiency number, useful for comparing combos within the same training type — not across different types, whose skills aren't equally valuable (see the salary multiplier table above: Jump Shot and Rebounding cost far more per point than Driving or Shot Blocking, so a high Sum on a cheap skill isn't the same win as the same Sum on an expensive one). Total pts/week is Sum × Players trained (per Tom: a 2-position combo trains roughly double the roster, 3-position triple, Team training the full five-player rotation) — a team-wide estimate, not a per-player rate, so it's only comparable across rows within the same training type where the underlying skills are worth the same.
