## Welcome

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/welcome_

Congratulations, you are now a general manager. Every new GM promises to turn the team around. Try to stop at 180 degrees.BuzzerBeater is a basketball-management game. You do not shoot, pass or argue with referees: you run a club. You choose the roster, set the tactics, plan the training, price the tickets, hire the staff and buy and sell players. The games are played by the game engine, which turns your decisions into possessions, shots and, if all goes well, wins. It takes no requests once the game starts, so the real work happens before tip-off.What winning looks likeYour club plays in a league, and leagues sit on levels of divisions, from Division I at the top downwards. A regular season is 22 league games. The four best teams in each conference reach the playoffs, and the division champion is promoted to a higher level, together with some teams that had the best regular-season records. At the other end, up to five teams per league go down. Climbing towards Division I is the long game, and it is measured in seasons, not weeks.There is plenty to win along the way. The best clubs in the world meet in BuzzerBeater's Best (B3), and the other teams compete in BuzzerBeater Madness, whose top-tier winner earns a place in the next B3. Trophies go to league and cup champions and runners-up, to B3 winners, to the winner and the runner-up of BuzzerBeater Madness, and to teams whose player is named league MVP. National teams, led by managers the community elects, play continental championships and World Championships, and your players can be called up.The rhythm of a weekTraining, the economy update and the daily update run on their own schedule, and your Calendar shows all of it in your own time zone. Tactics and ticket prices must be set at least 15 minutes before a game.How to read this manualStart here. These eight short sections give you the whole picture: this page, Your first week, The season, step by step, How a game is decided, Building a team, Money in a nutshell, Prizes, goals and milestones and Ten classic rookie mistakes. The Rules & reference sections that follow are the full rulebook, with every number and exception; go there when you need the details. Inside them you will find two kinds of notes. Rookie tips spell out what new managers most often get wrong. Pro tips are for managers who know the basics and want an edge. Lost on a screen? The ? in the top bar opens the section of this manual that explains it.Before anything else, skim the Rules of Conduct: one team per person (a supporter may add a second team, in Utopia only), no moving money between friends through inflated transfers, and nobody else logs into your account. Bans are permanent, and successful appeals are very rare.

## Your first week

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/first-week_

Your first week is about learning where things are without breaking anything. Nobody wins the league on day one. Here is a sensible order.Start at Home. You land there every time you open the game: your live or next game, your calendar and whatever needs your attention right now. Then read the Headlines in PR: they tell you what is happening at your club and in your league. See News.Meet your players. Open the Roster, sort it and read each player's skills; Ratings explains what every skill does. Do not judge anyone by DMI: the manual itself calls it very rough.Check the clock. Your Calendar shows games, training and the economy update in your own time zone. Anything you want to change for a game must be in at least 15 minutes before tip-off (2 minutes for a scrimmage). See Weekly Schedule.Set your lineup. Open your next game from the Calendar (or go to Tactics) and choose the 12 players who dress, your starters, backups and reserves, and an offense and a defense. The defaults are not a plan. Put at least three starters in the depth chart, or the coach may redo the lineup. See Games and Tactics.Choose your training. Pick a training area and the positions to train, then make sure those players will play: 48 minutes a week for ages 20 to 26, 45 for 18 and 19, 40 for 27 and older. See Training.Book a scrimmage. A club still in the cup cannot book one for a week its cup plays a round, from the opening round to the final; the weeks before and after are free. Find an opponent in Friendlies, under Competitions, or accept an offer another club sent you. It is an extra game of training and experience. Bookings are closed from Wednesday 17:00 to Saturday 03:00 UTC. See Scrimmages.Read the Economy page. Compare weekly salaries with weekly income and learn the difference between Current and Available Balance. New teams get $50,000 a week for four weeks, and a manager's first club pays no salary floor for 24 weeks: breathing room, not a fortune. See The Economy.Visit your arena. Look at attendance by seat type after your first home games before touching prices or seats. See The Arena.Say hello. Your league has a chat room of its own, on the CHAT tab of My League, and the world room is on Live Activity. Plenty of managers there enjoy answering questions, and the friendly ones can go straight onto your Buddy List. See Chat Rooms.What not to do yetDo not build Team Infrastructure. The manual calls it a luxury not intended for fresh teams, with steep building costs and weekly maintenance.Do not expand the arena on a hunch. Wait until sections sell out. Construction takes time, and the estimates are optimistic.Firing staff is not free. You pay one week at the staff member's next salary as severance. A Doctor, Trainer or PR Manager you fire is replaced at once by one of minimal skill; a secondary role is left empty. See Staff.Do not sell half the roster. Sale fees are lowest on players you have owned for a long time and climb the more you have sold recently, and some fans hate seeing their favourites listed. See Transfer List.Do not bid money you cannot spare. Bids cannot be retracted, and debt costs 5% a week.Do not call Crunch Time every game. Each one needs a week or two of recovery.Game Shape counts from day one. There is no grace week: your very first training update already uses the minutes your players have played since the club was set up. Only the first training update of each season is different: it resets everyone to the same starting Game Shape.That is plenty for week one. Before you set your second lineup, read How a game is decided; the tactics screen will look a lot less like a slot machine.Learn one step at a time with BuzzWhen the first-steps guide is offered on Home, Buzz shows the next action. Confirm the short introductory practice for a young player to see a real saved improvement, then prepare your lineup and ordinary weekly plan. The practice is available for the first 14 days after the welcome club is assigned and can be completed once per account. You can dismiss the guide; the existing new-team cash bonus does not depend on completing it. See Training and The Arena.

## The season

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/season-cycle_

Your Calendar shows every date in your own time zone; this page explains what each phase is for.You meet every team of your conference twice and every team of the other conference once.Rookie tipOpen the Calendar at the start of every week. It is the only place that shows every game, training and update in your own time zone, and each round's date matters: tactics lock 15 minutes before tip-off.A panel of reporters picks an All-Star team for each conference, the best two players per position, and the game is played in the biggest arena of the country among clubs with nobody in it, after a three-point contest. The cup, scrimmages, B3 and BBM carry on. See All-Star Game.When the last round has been played the table is final and the MVP vote opens.Places 5 to 7 play a best-of-three play-down to stay in the league, and the 8th goes down directly. The champion is promoted, and the places the division above leaves free go to the best of the other clubs by record: the first seeds first, then the wins, then the point difference. See Standings.From now on a club pays its wages at a weekly update only if it has a playoff or play-down game in the week that follows, so clubs with nothing left to play pay none. The update after the finals charges everyone again, as the next week opens the new season.Rookie tipOut of the race early? The wage-free playoff weeks are a good time to sell the players you will not need next season, and to give your youngsters scrimmage minutes.The night the season turnsTrophies go to the champions and runners-up, and the MVP vote is counted.The draft runs: three rounds, worst regular-season record first.Clubs are promoted and relegated, and the season's prizes and bonuses are paid.Every player turns a year older, injuries are cleared, game shape is reset and salaries are recalculated from current ratings.Team spirit and fan spirit return to normal; season tickets go up after a promotion and down after a relegation.The new leagues and schedules are created, and the first league round is played that same evening.Rookie tipThere is no break between seasons.Transfers, training and scrimmages never stop: nothing in BuzzerBeater closes between seasons.

## How games work

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/how-games-work_

Charles has a point, but the engine judges shot quality long before the ball reaches the rim. The manual gives the general framework, not the formulas, and so does this page. The box score shows only a fraction of it.A game is a string of possessionsA game is a series of opportunities to score. The kind and quality of each look depend on your offense and on the matchups between your players and their defenders. The player then decides whether the chance is good enough, depending on the shot clock, his experience, the score, the shots the team has seen recently and the offense itself. Whether the shot falls depends on the shooter and his team against the defender and his team: a great player who is the only threat on a bad team draws more coverage and shoots worse.Team ratings: players at positionsFor every match your team gets ratings that the engine uses on each possession, built from your players at their positions. Each position counts differently.Outside scoring and defense: both guards heavily, small forward medium, power forward slightly.Inside scoring and defense: center heavily, power forward somewhat heavily, small forward medium.Rebounding: center and power forward heavily, small forward somewhat heavily, guards medium.Offensive flow, which produces assists and avoids turnovers: point guard very heavily, shooting guard and small forward medium.So place players where their skills count, whatever their listed position. Rebounding limits the other team's chances and adds to yours; handling, outside defense and efficient scoring keep turnovers down.Pace and focusEvery offense mixes two dials. Pace is faster, slower or normal: faster means lower standards for taking a shot and more possessions; slower usually means better shots, with the odd bad one forced at the end of the shot clock. Fewer possessions also mean lower scores, which lets a weaker team stay closer to a stronger one. Focus is inside, outside or normal: it improves your looks in one area, worsens them in the other and shifts where the shots come from. The gain is usually outweighed by the loss, so focus only where you really have an advantage.Defenses trade too: zones buy defense in one area by giving it up in another, and the Full Court Press forces turnovers but costs stamina, defense and rebounding, and gives good ball handlers easy looks.Reading the opponentWith Game-Day Preparation you can predict the opponent's focus and pace: guess right for a sizeable edge, guess wrong and your players are unprepared, and a complete misread costs more than a near miss. Once the game starts, the coach adjusts only a little, and any change away from your tactics works less well than choosing it from the start. After the game, the MATCHUP RATINGS of the match recap tell you how many points your players at each position would have scored, on average, on 100 shots.Enthusiasm, shape and fatigueEnthusiasm is how hard your team can play. Crunch Time makes them try harder today but needs a week or two of recovery; Take It Easy saves energy for later. You pick it as the attitude of your tactic, in the editor's Allstar view. Enthusiasm drifts back towards 5 and reaches 5 every offseason; the biggest games offer only the two playoff options, and friendlies, private leagues, BuzzerBeater's Best and Madness are always played at Normal. Game Shape reflects how sharp a player looked in practice and suffers if he plays too little or too much. Stamina decides how fast players tire, and players aged 27 or older who play most of a game without real rest can become exhausted in the fourth quarter. Minutes also decide who trains, so read Building a team before you draw up the depth chart.ChemistryChemistry reflects how settled each player is at his current club. New signings settle in gradually, while established players are a little better on offense. It gives squad continuity a small benefit alongside skills, Game Shape and tactics; a new signing can still improve your team straight away (see Ratings).Chemistry: How settled a player is at his club, from 0% on the day he arrives to 100% after a full season there. Players the club drafted, and the squad a club starts with, are fully settled from day one. In club games all of a settled player's offensive skills are up to 5% better than a new arrival's, so a lineup that has played together for a season has an edge over one full of new signings. National team games ignore it. Like Game Shape, everyone can see it.Progress depends on time at the club, not minutes played.A transfer restarts settling-in at the new club. Chemistry is individual: a new signing does not lower his teammates' chemistry.

## Building a team

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/building-a-team_

A team is five positions, a bench and a plan for next season. Here are the tools.Skills and ratingsPlayers are rated in twelve skills. Jump Shot makes jump shots and Jump Range keeps them falling from further out; Handling, Driving and Passing create shots and protect the ball; Outside Defense guards the perimeter. Inside Shot, Inside Defense, Rebounding and Shot Blocking are the work near the basket. Stamina decides how long a player stays effective, and Free Throw helps everybody. Match skills to positions using the team ratings in How a game is decided: outside skills count most at guard, inside skills at center and power forward.DMI and the OVR on the player cards are very rough guides, in the manual's own words. Use DMI or OVR to check that a player is getting training, and play people where they help the team. Game Shape changes from week to week and tells you how well a player is likely to play right now.Age, potential and trainingYounger players improve faster than older ones, and taller and shorter players each train faster in different areas. Potential is a soft cap: a player who reaches it still improves, but much more slowly. So a young player well below his potential is the best home for your training, and a veteran at his cap is someone you play, not someone you train.Each week, on the Trainings page, you pick a training type and the positions to train. One or two key players improve rapidly; three or more positions spread the effect thin. Training pays in full only if the player plays at a trained position: 48 minutes a week for ages 20 to 26, 45 for 18 and 19, 40 for 27 and older. Less still helps, just more slowly. The depth chart and the Follow Depth Chart setting deliver those minutes, and scrimmages and cup games add more; pickup games, private leagues, BuzzerBeater's Best, the All-Star Game and national-team games do not count. Team Training, Free Throws and Stamina train the whole roster, playing or not. Skilled players learn related skills faster, and cross training spreads some of every week into other skills.The draftEvery offseason each league drafts a class of 48 young players, over three rounds. On the Draft & Scouting page you set a weekly scouting budget, and your scouts earn those points week by week: scouting a prospect once costs 1 point, interviewing him 2 more, and only an interview gives a very good read on his skills and potential. Unused points carry over. The worst records pick first, each forfeit costs a place, and your own order counts only once you submit your ranking.The transfer listYou cannot train everyone yourself, so you will buy. The Player Market is a first-price auction: you pay what you bid (with an auto-bid, the price the bidding reached), bids cannot be withdrawn, and the money is held while you lead. Each bid extends the auction until three minutes pass without a new one. Highly paid players refuse to join low-ranked teams: when your world rank is too low for a player's salary, his auction tells you the rank you need and the highest salary you can take on. When you sell, fees fall the longer you have owned the player and the fewer sales you have made in the past 14 weeks. And every player carries a weekly salary, so read it before the price tag.Think in seasonsThe manual's own advanced plan: develop more players than you can use, sell the extras to weaker teams and spend the cash on the positions you lack. Fans complicate it. Some love homegrown players and hate seeing favourites listed, others criticise a stagnant roster, and good domestic and home-drafted players sell more jerseys. Build a core, train it, and change it on purpose.

## Money

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/money_

Money keeps a team together, and the bank is not sentimental. The Economy page lists your recent income and expenses. Two numbers matter most: the current account balance, what you have, and the available cash, what you can spend, which adds the money your sales are due to bring and sets aside your leading bids on the transfer list.Where money comes fromTickets. Happier fans buy more tickets and the most expensive seats they can afford. Scrimmages are free for fans, cup games bring no arena revenue, and ticket money from playoff and relegation games is split half and half with the visitors.Merchandise. Jerseys sell when you win, when you have good domestic and home-drafted players, when your players played for their national team that week, and when you have league leaders.Television. Your league shares television revenue: every club collects a weekly TV contract, set when the season begins.Prizes and bonuses. Cash for each National Tournament, B3 or BuzzerBeater Madness game you win, a fixed award for the league champion and runner-up, and a one-time bonus when you are promoted. New teams also get $50,000 a week for their first four weeks.Transfers. Sales go straight into your balance the moment they complete, minus the sales tax.Where it goesPlayer salaries, every week, with relief in the playoff weeks once your season is over.Staff salaries, which creep up every week.Scouting, arena construction, SportCity buildings (building costs plus $5000 a week per level) and severance when you change staff.Purchases on the transfer list.The rules that biteThe TV deal comes with a salary floor: a minimum total salary, set as a share of TV money by division, charged even if your players earn less. A manager's first club is exempt for its first 24 weeks. A second, third or fourth team is not: it pays 20% of its floor from its first week, 20% more every four weeks, and the full floor after 16 weeks.Debt costs 5% a week. End four weekly economic updates in a row below -$500,000 and the club goes bankrupt: the whole squad is let go and replaced by a new one, and the books restart at $500,000. You keep the club, not the players. Debt interest and the bankruptcy count look at your balance plus the money your sales are due to bring, minus your leading bids: if a purchase settles before your sales do and your cash dips below zero, those sales still count.Being rich is not safe either. Every dollar above $25 million is taxed 10% a week, teams that spend much more than they earn pay an overextension tax, and fans boycott a team still waiting for its first league win when a third of the season ends, cutting ticket sales by up to 30% depending on how much money you hold. Goliath gets no sympathy, from the tax office or from the stands.The arenaTicket prices are yours to set within the league's ranges: bleachers $5 to $20, lower tier $18 to $70, courtside $50 to $200, luxury boxes $400 to $1600. The expensive seats sell win or lose but care about price; the cheap ones fill with rabid fans who come when you win. Expand when sections sell out, and remember that new seats take a day of construction for every 500. A bigger arena attracts sponsors and a Merchandise Store sells shirts, but both take a long time to pay for themselves.

## Prizes & goals

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/season-rewards_

Winning your league is not the only thing the game rewards. This page lists everything a season can bring you, what it is worth and where to find it.End of the season: prizes and promotionWhen the league finals are over, the champion and the runner-up of every league receive a cash award, and every club that moves up receives a promotion bonus. The bonus depends on the division you move into, and a champion who is promoted collects both. The award is paid with the game that decides the final and is counted in that game's income: on top of your share of the ticket money or, when the final goes to a third game, in its place, because the third game of a final earns no ticket money. The promotion bonus arrives with the offseason update, and the next time you open your club a celebration shows what you won. Only leagues pay a season-end prize: the cup, B3, BBM and private leagues do not, although the cup pays for every round you win.Division I: champion $400,000, runner-up $200,000, promotion into it $1,500,000.Division II: champion $250,000, runner-up $125,000, promotion into it $750,000.Division III: champion $150,000, runner-up $75,000, promotion into it $450,000.Division IV: champion $100,000, runner-up $50,000, promotion into it $300,000.Division V: champion $80,000, runner-up $40,000, promotion into it $225,000.Division VI and below: champion $60,000, runner-up $30,000, promotion into it $225,000.Rookie tipThe promotion bonus is the biggest single payment most clubs ever receive. Save some of it: in the higher division the opponents are stronger and the salary floor is higher.The MVP voteFrom the third game of the season, League › Leaders shows the MVP race live: the best seasons so far, ranked after every game, with who climbed since your last visit. When every regular-season game of your league has been played, the vote for its most valuable player opens there. The candidates are the 15 best players of the season among those who played at least half as many games as the busiest player. Every manager whose club played that season casts one ballot of up to three names, worth 6, 4 and 3 points, and may change it until the offseason counts the votes. You cannot vote for your own players. A club that does not vote is replaced by a judge, who ranks five candidates on their numbers.The winner's club receives the MVP trophy on its team page, and the extra merchandise income that comes with it the following season.Pro tipVote even when none of your players is in the race. Otherwise a judge fills in your ballot from the box scores, and managers who know the league often see what the numbers miss.Your weekly streakA week counts when, between Monday and Sunday (UTC), you both visit the game and save a tactic for one of your clubs. Weeks in a row make your streak; miss one and it starts again from zero, though your best streak is kept. If the week is still open on Saturday afternoon, a reminder tells you so.AchievementsAchievements mark things you did rather than things you won: an unbeaten regular season, a blowout, a quarter where the opponent barely scored, a big sale, a very young roster, years with the same club and many more. Most have several levels. They pay no money: each level is worth points, more for the rarer ones, and the points rank you in your league, your country and the world. You will find them on your profile.Played the original BuzzerBeater? In Settings › Personal Info you can prove your old account with its login name and a read-only BBAPI access code, and receive the Veteran achievement: one level for 1, 5, 10 or 20 seasons of your old club. The code is checked once and never stored, and each old club can be claimed only once.More to celebrateRetired numbers. Once a season you can retire the jersey number of a former player who spent three or more seasons with your club or won a title with it. The jersey hangs in Team HQ › History › Legacy.Rivals. You can declare one rival club each season, among the other clubs of your league group. Games against it count double in your rivalry record and are more likely to be televised, and a televised league game gives your players double experience. Your fans take your declared rival as theirs in the Fan Survey.Buzzer beaters. A game decided by a shot at the buzzer gets its own replay and a notice for both managers.Private leagues. Their champions win the title and a headline, but no trophy or money.

## Rookie mistakes

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/rookie-mistakes_

Every veteran has made at least half of these; some managed all ten in one season. Reading about them is cheaper.Forgetting to set tactics. The defaults are not a plan, and tactics lock 15 minutes before tip-off. Set every lineup early, cup games included. See Games.Training players who do not play. Training needs in-game minutes, and a trainee who sits improves slowly. The depth chart is part of your training plan. See Training.Crunch Time as a lifestyle. Each one costs a week or two of recovery, and the cup draws on the same enthusiasm as the league. Save it for games that matter. See Tactics.Believing DMI. The manual says it is a lot less meaningful than it first appears. Read the skills. See Ratings.Dressing fewer than three players. That is a 50-0 forfeit, no training minutes for that game, a worse tie-breaker and a lower draft pick. See Tactics.Firing staff to save money. An essential role is refilled at once with someone of minimal skill. Essential staff are called essential for a reason. See Staff.Bidding with money you need. Bids cannot be retracted. Debt costs 5% a week, and a club that stays below -$500,000 for four weeks goes bankrupt: its whole squad is let go and replaced by a fresh one. See The Economy.Helping a friend with a generous bid. Bidding above market to move money gets both teams fined or banned, and deals between friends, relatives or colleagues get a close look. See Rules of Conduct.Buying luxuries first. Team Infrastructure is not meant for fresh teams, and changing an arena design is never reimbursed. Win games first, build monuments later. See The Arena.Playing the starters every minute. Too many minutes hurt Game Shape, three-game weeks call for rotation, and players 27 and older can become exhausted in the fourth quarter. See The Game Engine.Honourable mentions: asking for scrimmages in Messages or in the public chat rooms, which counts as spam, and listing a player at a starting price you would hate to accept, because server crashes happen and finished sales are never undone.

## Coach Buzz

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/coach-buzz_

Every club in BuzzerBeater comes with an assistant coach: Buzz. He shows you around on your first day, picks the tactic for your welcome friendly because you have only just arrived, and stays on the bench for as long as you run the club. He answers to Buzz, to Coach and, after a heavy defeat, to "the one with the whistle".A shot from half courtNobody remembers his real first name any more. As a kid he fetched the balls in a small-town gym, then he kept the stats, then he became the twelfth man, sent in for the last few seconds when the game was already decided. In one of those games, with the clock running out, he let fly from behind the half-court line. It went in. It was the only shot he took all season, and it gave him his name for life: Buzz, as in the buzzer. He has been telling the story ever since, and the shot gets a step longer every year.Some veterans swear that his full name is Buzz R. Beater. He has never confirmed it. He has never denied it either.Knees, whistle and clipboardHis playing days ended when his knees filed a formal complaint. He stayed in the gym anyway: he carried the water bottles, filmed games from the top row of the stands, scouted opponents from the back seat of a bus, and in the end sat down beside the head coach. Now in his late thirties, he has seen every kind of season, from a promotion won with a week to spare to twenty defeats in a row. He still keeps the scoresheet of the twentieth; he says it keeps him humble.You will know him at once: a mop of black curls, a black tracksuit with orange stripes and the BB on the chest, a whistle on an orange lanyard that he never quite puts away, and a clipboard under his arm. On the clipboard there is a play he drew years ago that no team has ever run. He is sure its night will come.Why he works for youBuzz has never wanted the head coach's chair, let alone yours. He likes being the one who notices things: the player running on empty, the ticket price that keeps seats empty, the tactic that looked brilliant on paper and lost by twenty. You make the decisions; he makes sure you have seen what he has seen. He says "we" when he talks about the club, calls you boss, and saves his best jokes for himself.In the game he walks new managers through their first days, has a word to say when something happens at the club, from a breakthrough in training to a painful defeat, and answers your questions whenever you tap his face at the top of the screen. He knows this manual by heart and your club inside out, but he cannot see other clubs' secrets and he never changes anything himself: he tells you where to click. If you would rather work alone, you can send him home under Settings › Preferences.What he believes inA season is long. A bad week is a bad week, not a verdict.Never say "promotion" out loud before it is certain. He calls it superstition; he means experience.The coffee from the office machine is terrible and essential. The machine has been about to be fixed for three seasons.Nobody leaves early in this building.Behind the curls, Buzz is the game's AI assistant coach. His answers come from this manual and from your own club, and when he does not know something he says so instead of guessing. Guessing, as he puts it, is how assistant coaches get fired.

## All-Star Game

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/all-star-game_

Overview:It all happens on the ALL-STAR tab of your league's hub (Competitions > My League).The All-Star players are picked on their performances during the season, by a panel of reporters who cast their votes. After the game, you can check out its BOX SCORE like any other match.Schedule of Events:The Three-Point Competition:You can watch it "live" on the THREE-POINT SHOOTOUT panel. Six of the best shooters among the players on the ballot each get 25 shots, in sets of 5. The last ball of each set, the money ball, is worth 2 points, while the other shots are worth 1 point; the legend under the rack tells made balls, money balls and misses apart. The two best players advance to the final round, where another 25 shots are taken with the same rules. In the case of a tie in the final, the finalist who shot first wins.The All Star Game:This game is all about fame and glory. Did your player make the top 10 in your conference? Can he get along with his chief rival from that other team enough to work together? The club with the biggest arena in the country that has no player in the game gets to host it. However, we have made it so that no team benefits from the All-Star game at all: injuries won’t be carried over to the league games, minutes played don’t count towards training or game shape, players earn no experience, and there is no arena revenue from the game.The League Break:The national tournament and scrimmages go on as usual: if you are out of the tournament, book a scrimmage to keep the training minutes coming.

## Arena

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/arena_

Here you'll find information about your team's home arena. We've tried to make the arena system as customizable as possible, so you're free to build additional seats into your arena and to change the selling price of each seat type to match the demand of your fans, or to rename your arena. Along with all of the information you have learned from the initial scouting, you now have a very good understanding of this player's current skills (grade rating) and potential abilities. ## Economy

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/economy_

Economy, under Manage Team, is the headquarters for your finances. Its OVERVIEW lists your recent income and expenses, including player salaries, staff salaries, gate receipts, merchandise revenue, transfers and scouting costs, and lets you filter them by category; BREAKDOWNS splits your salaries and your merchandise line by line.There are several ways in which you can make use of this information. Primarily, it's a good reference for how your player and staff salaries compare to your total revenue, so you can determine whether you're spending beyond your budget. Your gate receipts, read together with the attendance by seat type in your arena's attendance history, can help you restructure and expand your arena to maximize your revenue. Two values are listed for your team's funds. The current account balance is the total amount of money you have in your bank, and the available cash is the amount you have to spend. The two differ while you have auctions running: the available cash adds what your own players on the transfer list are due to bring once someone has bid on them, after the sales tax, and sets aside what your leading bids on other players would cost. You can spend a sale's money before it arrives, so your balance can dip below $0 for a while if a purchase settles before your sale does.Economy also shows a weekly snapshot of your income and expenses side by side with the average club in your league, so you can tell at a glance whether you are running a tighter ship than your neighbours.Merchandising income comes from your fans buying team apparel, with a little help from your arena's sponsors and, if you built one, your Merchandise Store; BREAKDOWNS shows each part. Jersey sales are affected by a combination of factors.One factor is whether you have a winning team. Nobody likes to wear the jersey when you're losing, do they?Other factors include how many domestic players you have on your team, and also how many were drafted by your own team, as fans like supporting local players more than imported talent. This is weighted by the overall skill of the local players, so stocking your team with local bench warmers won't do you much good.

Players of any nationality earn a loyalty bonus after three full consecutive seasons at the club: 15% after three, then 15 more percentage points per full season, up to 75% after seven. Recorded past national-team appearances, All-Star selections, individual awards and participation in title-winning campaigns add a career bonus capped at 3%. Both rates apply to each player's salary-weighted share of base club merchandising, shown in their jersey sales. Transfers reset loyalty; career recognition follows the player. Current-season honours start counting towards career recognition from the following season.

How the bonus is calculated. Loyalty and career recognition add income; the leaderboard and national-team payment rates are not reduced to offset them. The bonus is applied to base club merchandising × the player's salary ÷ total roster salaries, not to the salary itself. It does not increase leaderboard bonuses, national-team appearance payments, club MVP payments, sponsorships or store income. The combined loyalty and career payment is limited to 5% of the player's weekly salary and rounded down to whole currency units.

Career recognition. A senior national-team debut adds 0.5% (U21 only: 0.25%, not added on top of the senior rate). All-Star selections add 0.5% per season, up to 1%. Each MVP award adds 1%; Rookie, Defensive Player and Sixth Man awards add 0.5% each. Each league, cup or BBM title adds 0.5%; B3 or continental titles add 1%; world titles add 2%. A team title counts only if the player played minutes in that winning competition and season. These contributions are added together, with the career total capped at 3%.

Examples. With $40,000 base merchandising, a player earning $100,000 in a $1,000,000 roster has a $4,000 share. After three full seasons, 15% loyalty adds $600 per economic update. At 75% loyalty plus 3% career recognition, he adds $3,120, below his $5,000 salary limit. A player earning $10,000 in a $100,000 roster has the same base share, but his combined history payment cannot exceed $500 per update. These percentages apply to the base share, not total merchandising.Pro tipDomestic players and your own draft picks sell more jerseys, weighted by how good they are. A strong local starter pays you twice, on the court and at the merchandise stand; a local bench warmer pays neither.Similarly, fans from around the country are particularly enthusiastic supporters of members of their national teams, and even more so when their national team is a successful one. So, if in a given week your player played in a national team game, fans from around the country will buy their jersey, typically in a quantity proportional to their salary. National team jersey sales will vary depending on the strength of the national team, but players playing in their domestic league tend to sell considerably more national team merchandise.Finally, fans from around the league tend to notice the league leaders, particularly in important categories such as points, rebounds, and assists but to some extent in all categories. If your team has players among the league leaders, you will see a bump in jersey sales from fans of other teams in your league, with an additional bump for the most eye-popping numbers in your country or in all of BuzzerBeater.As part of the agreement giving you shared television revenue, you also agree to pay a minimum total salary, shown on the Economy page. If your players earn less than this total, this minimum salary will be deducted anyway. The salary floor depends upon your league's television revenue:Division I: 241.5% of TV moneyDivision II: 187% of TV moneyDivision III: 130% of TV moneyDivision IV: 100% of TV moneyDivision V,VI: 70% of TV moneyThe salary floor does not apply to a manager's first club for its first 24 weeks. A second, third or fourth team pays part of it from the start instead: 20% in its first four weeks, 40% in weeks 5 to 8, 60% in weeks 9 to 12, 80% in weeks 13 to 16 and the full floor from week 17.Players are paid every week, except at the end of the season: from the first economic update after the last league round, a team pays its salaries only if it has a playoff or relegation game in the week that follows. A team whose season is over pays nothing. The update after the finals charges every team again, because the week that follows opens the new season.If you go into debt, you will be charged a 5% penalty each week until you are positive again. When the weekly economic update starts, the bank takes your balance plus the money your sales are due to bring, minus your leading bids: the penalty is worked out on that figure, every week it is below -$500,000 counts against you, and a week at or above that mark wipes the count. An advance is unavailable while a purchase remains pending; rebuilding also requires no pending sales.Path 1: a repayable sponsor advanceBuzz may find a sponsor willing to advance the minimum amount the club needs, up to $500,000. This is a debt, not a gift. The dated forecast must stay at or above -$400,000 and show a sustainable recurring budget. It tests gate receipts and merchandise with a 20% reduction, excludes future new-manager bonuses and unsettled sale proceeds, includes debt interest and known next-season wages, and checks the next 14 economic updates, including four-update operating windows. A structurally losing budget, missing data or a larger funding gap can make an advance unavailable. Reduce costs or consider rebuilding.Confirm the current quoted amount before receiving it. Prices, wages and commitments can change the quote. The Economy ledger records the advance and repayments separately from normal revenue. While the principal is outstanding, new player and staff purchases, internal staff appointments, paid starter offers, arena expansion or design purchases, paid club renaming, infrastructure expansion and increases in weekly scouting are blocked. Cost reductions, player sales, normal matches and training remain available.Repayment is automatic: 50% of positive recurring operating cash, plus net proceeds from player sales and infrastructure or arena reductions, can repay the advance. Repayments never exceed the remaining principal and cannot take cash below the protected operating reserve. Temporary opening bonuses are excluded from the operating surplus. For example, a $40,000 operating surplus can repay $20,000 if enough cash remains above the reserve. A $100,000 sale may repay only $50,000 if that is all the cash available above the reserve. The spending restrictions end when the advance is fully repaid.Path 2: rebuild the clubRebuilding is a separate, explicit confirmation. Keep the club identity, arena, fans and history; the staff and squad are rebuilt. You may retain up to two eligible training projects: players drafted by the club or never transferred, trained at your club in at least four recorded sessions during your current tenure, and not for sale. A sufficiently supported conservative market estimate is required. Players without a reliable estimate cannot be retained. Purchased players are excluded from this version of the programme.The combined retained value cannot exceed $300,000 and is deducted from the $500,000 opening cash. Each retained player replaces a slot in the new 18-player squad. For example, retaining two players valued at $120,000 in total leaves $380,000 cash and 16 newly generated players. The resulting payroll must pass the sustainability check; if it fails, nothing is changed and you must review the selection. Before confirming, review every player that will be kept or released and the resulting opening cash. Released players and the rebuilt staff cannot be restored.If the recovery failsRecovery does not guarantee survival and does not erase an outstanding sponsor advance. After preparation expires or an accepted plan fails, the ordinary rule still applies: four consecutive checks strictly below -$500,000 trigger bankruptcy; a check at or above that balance clears the counter. Ordinary bankruptcy replaces the entire squad. An outstanding advance survives resets and reassignment of the main club, and available restart cash above the reserve can go towards repayment. Once you accept an advance, you cannot later claim the subsidised option to retain players. There is no second sponsor rescue.Rookie tipRookie checklist: reduce recurring costs first, settle existing commitments, read the confirmation and choose before the preparation window closes. Buzz helps you rebuild a sustainable club; the advance must be repaid and the opportunity can be used only once.

## Fan Survey

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/fan-survey_

When BuzzerBeater fans don't like the way you are running your team, they are less likely to buy merchandise and attend your games. Even worse, some of them will change loyalties and support competing teams! Conversely, fans will reward a well-run team with their attention, and their money. A skilled PR-Manager may be able to help fans focus more on your team's achievements, and less on its shortcomings.Three times per week, on Wednesday, Friday and Sunday, your team's PR-manager conducts a survey of fans, and you can read it in the FAN SURVEY tab of PR. As the survey is based on a small sample of fans, beware that the results may not be completely accurate. The survey consists of 9 standard questions, and the responses are tallied into a single rating, ranging from 0 to 5 basketballs.Questions 1 and 2 ask about the team's performance during the previous season, and the current season. Whether your team went winless or won the championship the previous year, fans tend to gradually forget about it, and turn their attention to the current season.Question 3 is about the current season's National Tournament. Beware that fans of top division teams tend to take early exits from the cup very badly.Questions 4-6 ask about the team's performance in recent games. BuzzerBeater fans are notoriously fickle, and their opinion of your team can swing dramatically on the outcome of a single game. In addition to the most recent league game, fans also form deep impressions based on your last televised game, and your last league or cup game against your rival. When you have declared a rival and it still plays in your league group, that is the club they mean; otherwise the fans choose a hated rival from your league themselves. Either way it is the rival shown on your team page, and your calendar marks games against it RIVAL.Questions 7 and 8 pertain directly to the general manager's actions. Some fans prefer you to win with homegrown talent, take a very long time to warm up to new acquisitions, and react very negatively when you place their favorite players on the transfer list. Other fans will criticize you for maintaining a stagnant roster and under-utilizing the transfer list. When considering the response to these two questions, it may be useful to remember the old adage, "you can't please all the people all of the time."Rookie tipSome fans want homegrown players and hate seeing favourites listed; others hate a roster that never changes. You cannot please them all, so avoid both extremes: no clearance sales, no museum.The final question is the most important, as it asks the fans whether they are generally happy with the team, its performance, its players, and its general manager.Fans will not tolerate long losing streaks, especially if a team has the funds to prevent them. If a third of the season ends (after 7, 15 and 22 league games) and your team is still waiting for its first league win, they will boycott it, effectively reducing the amount of sold tickets of upcoming home games! This means that tickets might be reduced by as much as 30%, depending on current team funds. More specifically, the percentages based on the funds are as follows:Under $500k = 0%$500k - 750k = 3%$750k - 1M = 6%$1M - 1,5M = 9%$1,5M - 2,25M = 12%$2,25M - 3M = 15%3M - 4M = 18%4M - 5M = 21%5M - 7M = 24%7M - 10M = 27%over 10M = 30%The effect of the boycott will be reduced by 3% after each regular-season home game, as the fans cool down. Should there be another boycott while the existing one still lasts, the effects will be added together (maxing out at 30%).The effect of the boycott is determined after each third of the season, based on the funds available at that moment.Pro tipThe boycott is sized by your funds at the moment each third of the season ends, and it stacks up to 30%. A rich team without a win in a third pays the most, so when a winless stretch looms, that is what Crunch Time is for.

## Game Engine

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/game-engine_

In order to better help you structure your team, we thought it would be a good idea to tell you the general framework under which the game engine works.The truth is that what you see in the box score, or the match report, is only a fraction of what is going on inside the game engine. This is true in the same way that listening to a play by play of a basketball game isn’t going to show you some of the dynamics of the game you would see if you were there in person.Rookie tipThe box score shows only a fraction of what happened. Judge a tactic over several games, with the replays and the team ratings, not by one final score.The central part of the game engine is how the half court offense/defense works. The general idea is that a team gets a series of opportunities to score. What kind, and what the quality of those opportunities are is a function of the offense they are running, the matchups between the offensive players and their defenders. A player must decide whether the opportunity presented is good enough to take a shot… this of course changes as a function of amongst other things… the shot clock, the players experience, the score of the game, the history of the quality of shots the team has seen recently, the offense the team is running, and whether that rookie shooting guard of yours thinks he knows better than the coach does how good he is at making jump shots.The offensive schemes that we give you can be broken down into combinations of two aspects, pace and focus. Pace is either faster, slower or normal, and focus is either inside, outside or normal. A faster pace means that the team will lower its standards for which shot to take and take less time to get it up the court…resulting in more possessions in the game. Slower pace means just the opposite. Having an inside focus both increases the overall quality of the looks you get near the basket and decreases the quality of looks you get away from the hoop. It also skews the distribution of looks that you get towards inside looks. Outside focus does exactly the same, but for longer range shots. In general the marginal gain you get is outweighed by the loss, so it only makes sense to focus one way or the other if you really feel you have an advantage in that area.Obviously the success of your team depends on other aspects of the game as well, such as the ability of your players to create opportunities for each other. The better offensive flow you have, the more high quality assisted shots you will get. Also, being able to rebound effectively will limit your opponents opportunities to score, and increase yours. Reducing turnovers and forcing turnovers is also important. The better handling you have, the better outside defense, and the more efficient you are at scoring (because the longer you take to score the more opportunities there are to turn it over) the better you will do in this department. Putting on a full court press will increase the number of turnovers you force, but will also give opponents with good ball handling skills easy looks at the hoop.During the course of the game, your coach will make adjustments as a result of how the game is going. If you are making a lot of inside shots, he will tell you to take more inside shots. If the opponent is making a lot of inside shots, he will tell your defenders to double-team in the post more often. However, any time that the coach makes a change away from the tactics you started out with, it will be a less effective than if you had simply chosen to double-team the post all along (i.e., you will find that it is easier for your coach to tell the defenders to focus even harder than they already are on inside defense if you are already playing a 2-3 zone). The closer your submitted tactics are to the optimal choice, the better, especially because the coach is only able to make limited adjustments once the game has started.While the adjustments and counter-adjustments are very complex, and the details alone take up over 10,000 lines of code (really!), we have tried to give you a little bit of a taste of this via the Matchup rating for each team.Player ExhaustionShort rosters have been addressed from a variety of angles, the latest being player exhaustion, which applies in every match except scrimmages. While it won't affect most managers at all, or only slightly, it stops short rosters from working as well as they used to at the top level of every competition.Overview of player exhaustion:It only affects players who are 27 years old or above.It can happen only after a player has already played most of the match without much rest (hasn't been subbed enough).It does not affect injuries nor does generate them.An exhausted player can continue playing (although the GE usually finds it better for them to sit).An exhausted player has their energy severely reduced so they perform at a much lower level than usual.An exhausted player can come back into the game, although they most likely will be suffering from some energy loss.Anytime in the 4th quarter if the player has not been subbed out enough* during the course of the game, any action he makes may cause him to become exhausted.* There is a specific ratio of rest a player needs to be safe from exhaustion. For example, Subbing for 30 seconds in the first 3 quarters is not enough.Rookie tipExhaustion only affects players 27 and older who have played most of the game without enough rest, and only in the fourth quarter. Thirty seconds on the bench in the first three quarters does not count as rest.The matchup rating represents the number of points that players at a given position would have scored, on average, if they were to take 100 shots; you find them as MATCHUP RATINGS in each game's recap. A higher number is indicative of better scoring opportunities at that position over the course of the game, and of course you should try to take full advantage of your strength when selecting your tactics. Since tactics can be very complicated, you should ask your fellow managers what they have learned, in your league's CHAT or in the world room on Live Activity: they can tell you a lot more from their own experience than we can in a brief description of the engine. Good luck!Pro tipA matchup rating is the points your players at a position would score, on average, on 100 shots. Compare them after each game: they show where your offense found its good looks, and where to aim the next game plan.Team fouls and free throwsTeam fouls follow the NBA rules. A team may commit four team fouls in a quarter and three in each overtime; from the next one on, every foul it commits in defense gives the other team two free throws, even when nobody was shooting. A team that reaches the last two minutes of a period below that limit has only one foul left to give: its second foul in those two minutes already sends the opponent to the line. Team fouls start again from zero in every quarter and every overtime, and a foul on a shot always gives free throws, whatever the count. On the scoreboard, BONUS appears next to a team once every foul it commits in defense gives free throws.ChemistryChemistry also contributes to club games. It rewards continuity with a small benefit to all offensive skills, without replacing player quality or tactical choices. For how it affects squad building, see How a game is decided.

## Games

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/games_

Your Calendar (in the sidebar, and on the CALENDAR tab of every team page) lists all of the matches of the current season involving your team, those already played as well as those scheduled for the future. You can look at a single day, the next 30 days or the whole season, and pick an earlier season to see its games. The order of a league's games is drawn anew every season, so last season's placings do not decide whom you open against or when you play at home.Every round of the league also puts one of its games on television. The league shares those slots out fairly, so every club gets a few televised games a season, and the broadcasters' first choice is a game between declared rivals. Your calendar marks those games TV as soon as the season's schedule is out.Pro tipA televised league game gives your players double experience, and it is the one your fans remember when they answer the Fan Survey. Being on TV does not sell tickets or jerseys by itself: the fans react to the result as they would to any other game. Plan your effort around those dates.Every game on the calendar opens its own page, and what you find there depends on when you look:Once a game has started, its page is the match viewer: watch it live, with running commentary, the score and updated stats for all of the players, or come back later and watch the replay of every shot, rebound, foul, timeout and point. A game on air also shows its running score wherever it appears, from your calendar to the top bar, and its own chat room runs beside the play-by-play.Before a game, its page is where you set its tactics: your active roster (the 12 players who will be eligible to play in the game), your starting lineup, your backups and reserve players, and the offensive and defensive tactics you will use. You can also prepare them in Tactics. If you don't set a game's tactics, your team plays its tactics for that competition or, failing those, its team default, which may not be the best choices for your team. Choosing your tactics to optimize your team's performance requires careful thought; much of this ability comes from trying out varying strategies and different player combinations, analyzing the resulting box scores, and talking with more experienced users. Tactics lock 15 minutes before tip-off (two minutes before a scrimmage), because after that, the coach will be down in the locker room giving his pre-game pep talk.Once a game is over, its page shows the final score, the complete box score and the team ratings for the match, and lets you watch the replay.Rookie tipTactics lock 15 minutes before tip-off (two minutes before a scrimmage), and a game you did not set up plays your fallback tactics: the ones for that competition, or your team default. Your Calendar shows every tip-off in your own time zone, so set lineups the day before instead of racing the clock.

## National Teams

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/national-teams_

ElectionsElections are held in order to decide who will become the manager of a national team. Elections last for one week, and you find them on the NATIONAL TEAMS tab of the country's hub (for your own country, Nations > My Country). Users may register for only one election at a time, but they may register in any country, including those not their own. The winner of the election is decided by the votes of other BuzzerBeater users: only users of that country can vote, and only once their account is four weeks old.National team elections have two distinct phases. In the first phase, which lasts for four days, interested candidates register for the managerial position and start to receive votes from the country's users. After the fourth day, if at least 10 votes have been cast, the field is cut: only the top five candidates (ties included) and anyone holding at least 5% of the votes stay in the race, a candidate without a single vote is out, and no new candidates can register. The election ends after three more days, with the candidate with the highest number of votes winning the managership. In the case of a tie, the candidate who has been playing BuzzerBeater for longer is declared to be the winner.Votes can be changed or canceled at any time. If the candidate that a user has voted for is culled from the election pool after the first phase, the user's vote is canceled, and he or she is welcome to vote for one of the remaining candidates.Information and debate about the candidates take place in the national team's CHAT room, open to the country's users, the team's manager and the candidates running in its election. The discussion is moderated by BuzzerBeater Game Masters, who may set detailed rules for each election.BuzzerBeater administrators (BBs) are not allowed to run in elections. Game Masters (GMs) are, although they are not allowed to moderate the discussion of an election in which they are registered.The winner of a National Team Manager election is elected for a two season term, with the relevant competitions outlined in the next section.CompetitionsThe national team competitions take place over the course of two seasons. During the first season, teams will compete in a continental championship tournament that also serves as the qualifying tournament for the World Championships. During the second season, the best teams will compete in the World ChampionshipsContinental qualifying takes place for the National Teams in odd-numbered seasons, and the World Championships take place in even-numbered seasons. Conversely, qualifying for the U21 teams takes place in even-numbered seasons, and the U21 World Championships take place in odd-numbered seasons.Past performance is used to seed teams, based upon the World Rankings. National team games tip off at the same time for every country, set by the national calendar; your Calendar shows it in your own time zone. Each continental championship is hosted by one of its teams, decided by the previous season's consolation tournament (see below), and the World Championship is hosted by one of the qualified nations. Its 16 places are filled in this order:The champion and the runner-up of each continental championship.The two beaten semifinalists of the European championship.The best-ranked remaining teams in the World Rankings, until the field is full.Africa Schedule: There are 8 teams in BuzzerBeater Africa. The 8 teams compete in a single round robin from week 2 to week 8. The top six teams then compete in another single round robin from week 9 to week 13, carrying forward only their games against one another. The top 4 teams advance to the semifinals on the Friday of week 13 and the final in week 14. The two finalists qualify for the World Championships. Every other team will play the consolation tournament.Americas Schedule: There are 21 teams in BuzzerBeater Americas. The 21 teams are drawn into three first-round groups of seven and play a round robin from week 2 to week 8. The top four teams from each group are placed into one of two second-round groups of six, with results not carrying forward, played from week 9 to week 13. The top two teams in each group advance to the semifinals on the Friday of week 13 and the final in week 14. The two finalists qualify for the World Championships.Asia Schedule: There are 22 teams in BuzzerBeater Asia. The 22 teams are drawn into three first-round groups (one of eight, two of seven) and play a round robin from week 2 to week 8. The top four teams from each group are placed into one of two second-round groups of six, with results not carrying forward, played from week 9 to week 13. The top two teams in each group advance to the semifinals on the Friday of week 13 and the final in week 14. The two finalists qualify for the World Championships.Europe Schedule: There are 47 teams in BuzzerBeater Europe. They are drawn into six first-round groups of eight (one of them with seven). Each group plays a round robin from week 2 to week 8. The top two teams advance into two second-round groups of six, with no teams from the same first-round group drawn into the same second-round group. Results do not carry forward from the first round to the second, which is played from week 9 to week 13. The top two teams in each group advance to the semifinals on the Friday of week 13 and the final in week 14. The four semifinalists qualify for the World Championships.Second Chance Qualifiers: no longer played. The World Championship places they used to hand out are now filled from the World Rankings.World Championships: 16 teams qualify for the World Championships, and are drawn into two first-round groups of eight. Each group plays a single round robin from week 2 until week 8. The top four teams of each group advance to the second round, where each plays the four from the other group once, carrying its whole first-round record forward, from week 9 until week 12. The top four teams advance to semifinals during week 13, with the 4th-placed team playing the 1st-placed team and the 3rd-placed team playing the 2nd-placed team. The final is played in week 14. The winning team will be the World Champions of BuzzerBeater for the next two seasons!Consolation TournamentNational teams that do not qualify for the World Championships will enter the Consolation Tournament. The Consolation Tournament will consist of two phases, the round-robin phase and the knockout phase. In the round-robin phase, each team will play 8 games. The team that advances the furthest from each confederation will host the next continental competition (See HCA Restrictions). In the event of multiple teams from the same confederation being knocked out in the same round, and being the final teams from that confederation alive, the highest seeded team will host the next continental competition. If no teams from a confederation reach the knockout phase, the team with the highest standing in the round-robin phase will host the next continental competition.Home Court Advantage RestrictionsIn our eyes, the HCA was never meant for the strongest nations, clearing their way to an easy trophy(the 1st place usually). Sometimes, in a funny way, a team would win the consolation, win the local championship that comes after that, and completely fail in the next local championship, leading to another consolation win and another HCA and another title in their local championship.- A national team (senior or U21) that finished in the top four of its continental championship in the past 6 seasons is passed over for hosting, even if it went furthest in the consolation tournament.StandingsEach national team competition has its own hub, which you reach from Nations > World > COMPETITIONS or from a country's hub. National teams cannot recruit players from teams that do not have active managers (i.e. bots). A National Team player cannot be fired directly, instead he can only be sold.A manager who stops logging in loses his club to the computer after six weeks (a Supporter keeps it however long he is away), but its players stay on their national teams. If a club is reset, its called-up players go to the transfer list rather than out of the game, and keep their call-up.National team managers can see the player ratings of any player on their national team, but cannot otherwise see player ratings they would not otherwise have access to.When a player is called up to his national team, his club's owner is notified and the call-up makes the club's Headlines.Having your player on a national team will not affect his player ratings or weekly minutes in any way. Owning internationals is good business.National Team EnthusiasmDismissing a player from a National Team causes a drop in the national team's enthusiasm; the more frequent the dismissals, the greater effect they have on enthusiasm. Adding players does not impact enthusiasm. Enthusiasm for National Teams rises (if below 5) or drops (if above 5) at a slower rate than they do for club teams.At the start of the competitions, the enthusiasm of every national team entered in the World Championship, a continental championship or the consolation tournament is reset to 5, in the second week of the season.Manager PopularityDissatisfied with your national team manager's performance? Express your opinion in the MANAGER APPROVAL card on the country's NATIONAL TEAMS tab, approving or disapproving of the way he or she has been handling the team. Only users of that country whose account is at least four weeks old can vote, and you can change your vote at any time. The approval rating is shown to everybody, and the votes start again from zero whenever the manager changes, re-election included. It is an opinion poll, though: a low rating does not remove the manager.RankingsThe world championships aren't the only way that national teams can compete for status. Every game of the World Championship, the continental championships and the consolation tournament hands out ranking points between the two teams, all of them counting the same; friendlies are worth nothing, and senior and U21 teams are ranked separately (see Nations > World > RANKINGS). All ranking points that national teams receive, they keep for 28 weeks (i.e., two seasons). Points expire on a rolling basis.The winning team gets a bigger share of the ranking points for a game, but it doesn't get all of them. Precisely how many points are received depends upon the relative strength of the two teams and on the margin of victory. If the teams are evenly matched, then the winning team will receive 75% of the points, and the losing team the other 25%. An underdog who scores an upset will get nearly 100%, but a strongly favored team that takes home a predictable victory will receive only a bare majority.Although it's usually safe to assume that the team with more ranking points will be the one that we deem stronger, the two are not identical. We track teams' strength in a separate internal variable that we don't publish (so don't ask!). The algorithm we use is called Glicko-2 and is similar to the systems used by Baseball Prospectus and by the US Chess Federation. If you're interested in the gory details, click here, but the math might be scary. Don't complain that we didn't warn you.National team staff and first assistantSenior and U21 national teams each have a manager, up to two assistants and up to twelve scouts. The manager appoints assistants. The manager and confirmed assistants manage scouts and prospect assignments; confirmed staff collaborate on prospect notes, tags and shortlists. Invitations must be accepted, and inherited staff wait for the new manager to confirm them before they can write. Scouting work belongs to the national team and survives a change of manager. Until the enthusiasm of the national teams is reset to 5 before the season's first national team game, confirmed assistants and scouts can also call up and dismiss players, so the staff can see the ratings of the players it follows; after that, only the manager can.Delegating match preparationThe elected manager can designate one confirmed assistant as first assistant in the national team’s STAFF tab, using Delegate match preparation. The manager does not have to be absent, and no absence timer or approval of each saved lineup is required. Both people sign in with their own accounts; never share sign-in credentials.The manager retains full control and responsibility for the national team, including the right to review and change a lineup before its lock deadline.The first assistant opens TACTICS from the national team’s hub or Prepare matches from Staff, and prepares that national team’s future matches using its already called-up players. The delegate can choose the game roster, starters, backups, positions, offense, defense, substitutions, effort and all other match settings available at their interface level. Other assistants and scouts gain no match-preparation permission.Saving together and reviewing changesThe editor uses each person’s normal manual-save or autosave preference. This history is visible only to the current manager and the currently delegated assistant, and is not published to opponents or the rest of the staff.If someone else saved a newer lineup after you loaded the editor, your older draft is refused rather than silently replacing their work. Reloading discards that local draft. A player who is 7'2" but has a low rebounding skill simply is very bad at positioning and will not get very many rebounds. He'll learn more quickly though...Shot Blocking: Will help to block shots. Not very useful for perimeter players.Stamina: A player with high stamina will tire more slowly, and can be effective for longer periods on the court.Free Throw: Will help to make free throws. National team games ignore it. Like Game Shape, everyone can see it.DMI and OVR: DMI and OVR are rough guides to a player's skills, not guarantees of how he will play. Use the skills and game log to judge where he helps your team.Best Position: The position at which a player's skills are worth the most, recalculated after every weekly training. ## Tactics

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/tactics_

Whether an outside shot goes in is a combination of the individual and team outside scoring rating, as well as the opposing individual and team outside defense. If a great player is the only option on a bad team, he will draw more coverage, and therefore his shooting percentage will go down; similarly a bad player on the same team will only tend to shoot outside shots when he is wide open. The same applies to other shot types.Tactical OptionsOffenseOffenses generally have an inside/outside/neutral focus and a faster/slower/normal pace compared to the base offense. They are not all equally faster/slower, as they're approximations of real offenses. A slower pace will often result in higher-quality shots, but occasionally will result in bad shots at the end of the shot clock. Fewer possessions in the game also means that scores will be lower, which means that bad teams can stay closer to good ones. An inside or outside focus means that teams will try to get more inside/outside shots, but the players will still take the best shots they can, and if the opposing inside defense is making it impossible to score near the basket, they won't attempt many inside shots no matter what you do. Normal Pace.Outside Isolation: Your team finds their best outside scorer. Normal Pace.Pro tipA slower pace means fewer possessions and lower scores, which keeps a weaker team closer to a stronger one. As the underdog, slow it down. ## Training

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/training_

Training MinutesHow many weekly minutes each age group need for full training?18-19 years old: 45 weekly minutes.20-26 years old: 48 weekly minutes.27+ years old: 40 weekly minutes.Note: One minute buffer is placed for all age groups, which means 44 minutes is also sufficient for 18-19 age group.In addition to playing games, attending scrimmages, and spending their hard-earned salaries, your players must also find time out of their busy schedules for practice.Again, we've tried to leave the tactics as open-ended as possible, so you can have a strong hand in specifying how your players will grow.On the Trainings page, the Weekly Training Plan starts with the Training Type, where you can select from one of many training areas. While many choices correspond directly to specific player attributes (such as Inside Defense and Rebounding), some will improve several complementary traits (like Outside Shooting and Ball Handling).Then, under Target, you choose which player positions to train, or the whole team for the drills that allow it; the plan counts once you confirm it. Players will only get better with a combination of out-of-game training and in-game experience, so you can have the coach choose to train one or two key players per week, who will then show a rapid improvement, or to train three or more positions with reduced effectiveness. Each drill is built for certain positions, and the plan shows how much of its effect it keeps on the ones you picked. Players should ideally get 48 minutes per week at the positions being trained, or one full game, in order to get the maximum benefit from their training. If they play more than 48 minutes, this is not a problem. If players play under 48 minutes, they will still improve, but less quickly than they would if they got more playing time. League, cup and playoff games and scrimmages all count; BuzzerBeater Madness, pickup games, private leagues, BuzzerBeater's Best, the All-Star Game and national-team games do not.Pro tipChoose your trainees first, then build the depth chart around their minutes. Follow Depth Chart keeps the coach from overruling you, and one or two key players improve far faster than three or more positions trained at once.The first three training types on the list, Team Training (which works on Game Shape), Free Throws and Stamina, work a little differently from the rest. For these attributes, it is easy for the coach to train all of the players on your team (he can have them shoot free throws for an hour or run laps around the building), and they don't depend too much on in-game practice. Therefore, selecting one of these options will train your entire roster, whether or not they play in any games that week.Rookie tipTeam Training, Free Throws and Stamina train everyone, minutes or not. Team Training also gives every player a small lift in Game Shape, bench included, on top of what his minutes earn. Many teams do their Free Throws in the offseason, when normal minutes are harder to get, but Team Training is a poor choice then, because Game Shape is not updated during offseason training.For the other training types, players will improve more quickly not only with additional playing time, but also a more skilled player will improve more quickly. For example, a great inside defender and rebounder will find it easier to improve his shot blocking than a poor inside defender and poor rebounder would. This encourages you to develop players with a combination of skills, but there are many combinations of skills which work well together -- you can develop players who will be a good fit for your team's needs.Additionally, each player has a potential which will determine the best that player can possibly become. Potential acts as a "soft" cap on ratings, meaning that a player who has trained to his full potential may still improve, but will improve much more slowly than a player who has not yet reached his potential.Something to keep in mind: as in the real world, younger players will improve more quickly than older ones, and taller players will train faster in some areas while shorter players improve faster in others. You'll have to take advantage of this information to optimize your training routine.Another important thing to keep in mind is cross training.This method emphasize each skill as part of becoming a more complete basketball player, and make reference to every skill. Thus, improvement in driving is related not just to related skills (like handling), but also more weakly to unrelated skills (like shot blocking). For the average player in the game, this will result in approximately 10% slower training in their primary skill than before, and additional training in other skills approximately corresponding to that 10% loss. A particularly well-rounded player will continue to receive cross-training but will see a much lower reduction in primary skill training, while a particularly one-dimensional player will see a larger loss. ## Transfer List

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/transfer-list_

The key to making a good team? Have good players. You can't train them all by yourself, so you'll need to pick up quite a few on the transfer list. Open the Player Market, a tab of the Market, to search the listed players: set a minimum and maximum level for up to four skills (supporters up to eight), and narrow the list further by age, height, salary, potential, game shape, experience, position, nationality or injuries. Then search to see the subset of the listed players matching your criteria; if you don't enter any restrictions, the entire list will be returned. You can save up to five searches to run again later, and the Bids tab keeps track of the auctions you are in.The transfer list works as a simple, first-price auction. That is, whatever value you enter as your bid, that's what you'll pay if you win the auction. You can also set an auto-bid: a private ceiling up to which the auction raises your bid for you, one step at a time, whenever a rival bids. Nobody else is told it exists, and if you win you pay only the price the bidding reached. Be mindful of your available funds when bidding; once you bid on a player, those funds are deducted from your available balance for as long as you are the high bidder, even though you might not end up winning the auction. The bank doesn't want you defaulting on your bid, so you can't use that money for other purposes. But don't worry - if someone else outbids you, you'll get your money back immediately.Rookie tipYour bid is what you pay, it cannot be retracted, and the money is locked while you lead. Plan with the Available cash line on the Economy page, not your cash reserves.When selling a player, some of the purchase price is used to pay agent fees and other taxes on the sale. The fees and taxes for a sale are determined by two factors: the amount of time that the player has been on your team, and the total number of sales your team has made in the previous 14 weeks. The fees are lowest when you have owned the player for a long time, and when you have made few sales in the preceding weeks. When you list a player from his page, the listing shows the sales tax that applies to him right now. You cannot offer a player for sale until he has been on your team for at least 4 days.We know that transmission delays can happen for a few seconds at the very end of an auction. Therefore, whenever a user places a bid on a player, the time remaining in an auction is extended so that three minutes must pass with no new bids before the auction is over.Now, we weren't born yesterday. We know that some people are going to try to transfer money from friends by purchasing atrocious players from them at exorbitant prices. We have some algorithms that check purchase prices for any suspect behavior, and users who keep other players honest. If you see someone you think is trying to transfer funds in this way, please report them with Report Cheaters in Settings. It makes the game more fun for everyone when the playing field is level.Suspicious sales can be held for up to 24 hours when their auction ends while the GMs review them, and a held sale can be cancelled: the player then stays with the seller and the bids are void. See the Rules of Conduct.Please note that once a player has been confirmed to be listed, he cannot be removed from the transfer list. Also, once a bid for a player has been confirmed, it cannot be retracted. A listing runs for three days; supporters can choose a longer one, up to seven.When you fire a player earning at least $2,500 a week, he goes on the transfer list as a free agent, with a starting price of ten times his salary (never more than $500,000); cheaper players simply leave the game, and either way you get nothing for him. ## Utopia League

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/utopia-league_

The "Utopia" league is a country within BuzzerBeater specifically created to house “second teams”, which are managed by existing users from all around the world: your first club always plays in a real country, and only then can you found one here. We wanted to give people a chance to participate in an engaging league if they feel they are not being challenged enough in their own league. Other users that feel that they can prove their managerial prowess by having another team and building it from the ground up also may find this feature interesting.The Utopia league indeed provides a very competitive environment, as it's filled with proactive managers with experience and ambition. A second team is reserved for Supporters: every manager runs one club, and a Supporter may found one more, in Utopia or in another country where he doesn't have a club yet. The “Utopia” league is the same as any other country, except for some specifics mentioned below. This means it has its own flag, its own cup and its own players.Here are some of the most important specifics of the new feature:OrganizationUtopia is organised like every other country: leagues of 16 teams in two conferences, stacked in divisions. A new Utopia team takes over a computer-run club in the lowest division, as it would anywhere else, and climbs from there. At the end of each season, the computer-run clubs of a division are the first to make room for the managed clubs coming up from below, so the higher divisions fill up with human teams as the country grows.Supporters OnlyA second team is one of the privileges of being a Supporter. Supporter status belongs to your account, not to a club, so both of your teams enjoy the Supporter features. Keeping second teams for Supporters serves the country's main goal: Utopia should be a highly competitive place in which every manager has a strong incentive to remain very active, and managers who support the game tend to be serious about their second team.National TeamThe Utopia league does not have its own National Team, as the country is composed of managerial talent from all over the world.B3 ParticipationUtopia's clubs are invited to the BuzzerBeater's Best like any other country's: its division I champion and the winner of the Utopia Cup, from the very first season. A manager plays only one team in B3, though: if both of your teams qualify, the better-ranked one takes part and the spot goes to the next team in line. In BuzzerBeater Madness, both of your teams play, even when they fall in the same tier.Transfer Market and DraftThe Utopia league has its own players drafted each season. These players can be sold to and bought by all teams in BuzzerBeater without restrictions, and the “Utopia” teams are free to buy and sell players from all other teams, with one exception: none of your clubs can bid on a player that another of your clubs owns now, or has owned since you took it over.Pro tipA Utopia team trades freely with the whole world except your other club: neither can bid on a player the other owns, or has owned since you took it over. Your two teams cannot feed each other.VisibilityYour manager profile lists all of your clubs, and when you take over a second one, the club you have held the longest announces it in its league's Headlines.ScrimmagesUtopian teams can engage in scrimmages as normal, one a week like every club. There is no special limit on games between your own two teams.CheatingIf a manager is banned from the game for violating BuzzerBeater’s rules of conduct, BOTH the original and the Utopian team will be locked, regardless of which team violated the rules.New Manager BonusA Utopia team is a new club like any other, so it receives the new manager bonus during its first weeks, however experienced its manager is.Game TimesUtopia keeps its own clock, like every country: its games are played at Utopia's game times, on UTC−8, whatever country your first club plays in. For many managers that means the middle of the night or the early morning, so check your Calendar, which shows every tip-off in your own time zone. We believe the proactive managers of Utopia league won’t have any problems handling this.You can change the time of your Utopia club’s home games. In Settings, under Preferences, the Game time list offers twenty hours, from Utopia’s standard game time back to nineteen hours earlier, each shown as a clock time in your own time zone. The choice applies to the club’s home league, playoff and cup games, and to the friendlies you book at its arena in a normal week; away games stay at the home club’s time. Games less than a week away are not moved, and a choice made now reaches the others a moment later, so the Calendar updates shortly after you save. Each club has its own choice: it returns to the standard game time when the club passes to a new manager.Promotion and DemotionUtopia promotes and relegates exactly like every other country: see Standings for how the playoffs and the relegation series work.

## Weekly Schedule

_Source: https://play.buzzerbeater.com/manual/weekly-schedule_

The important events of your upcoming week — games, the daily update, the economy update and training — are listed in your Calendar, in your own time zone.It can hold more than your own team: use Follow calendar on another team's page, a manager's profile, a competition or a single game to add its games to yours, and add events of your own for anything else you want to remember.
