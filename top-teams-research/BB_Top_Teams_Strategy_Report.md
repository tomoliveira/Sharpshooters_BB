# How long-run BuzzerBeater winners are built

Research for **Sharpshooters** (#32988, League III.15, Brasil). Compiled 2026-09-24 from public buzzerbeater.com pages (team history, transfer history, season stats, player history and overview pages). All reads were read-only.

Lines marked **[Inferrence]** are my interpretation, not something the site states.

Teams studied:

| Team | ID | Country | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Venomous Scorpions | 56709 | Iran | World #24, 10 league titles, 3 B3 titles, dominant again in S72–73 |
| I Love Tehran | 146725 | Iran | One of only 2 teams with 3+ B3 titles since 2017 (S39–41) |
| Młoty Stargard | 162788 | Polska | The other one (S44, S45, S47). World #14 |

## 1. B3 winners since 2017

Source: [BuzzerBeater's Best history](https://buzzerbeater.com/world/overview.aspx), checked by team ID.

- From S38 (the first season starting in 2017) to S72, only **two teams won 3 or more B3 titles**: I Love Tehran (S39, S40, S41) and Młoty Stargard (S44, S45, S47). Both have 4 all-time.
- Five teams won two: dequestion (S71–72), Etonic Akeem (S64–65), bigshoes (S62–63), Goldsons (S52–53), Sant Mateu (S51, S67).
- No team has won more than two since S48.

## 2. Venomous Scorpions: boom and bust cycles

**Results:**
- 10 Bartar titles: S10, S17, S24, S34–36, S50–52, S72.
- 13 Iran Cups and 3 B3 titles (S16, S24, S50).
- 7 relegations: S6, S12, S20, S27, S38, S55, S57.

**Spending comes in bursts, and titles follow:**

| Burst | Result | Afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| 2009 | S10 title | Relegated S12 |
| 2011 | S16–17 | Relegated S20 |
| 2013 | S24 treble | Relegated S27 |
| 2016 ($38.3M) | S34–36 | Relegated S38 |
| 2020 | S50–52 | Relegated S55 |
| 2022 | Contender S59–71 | — |
| 2026 | S72 treble | — |

Over their whole history: $214.7M sold against $230.8M bought, so a net of −$16.1M.

**Core payroll** (weekly salaries of the 8 best-paid players) peaks at $1.0M–$2.15M, then drops **75–95%**. The low point was $56k in S38–39.

**Since 2017 the model has two parts:**

1. **Prospects.** They buy 18-year-olds with hall of famer or all-time great potential, still on $3k–$4k salaries, for $1.5M–$3M, and train them for about 10 seasons.
   - 2017: Dehelean and Újszászi.
   - 2018: Droniou, about 22 years old on $20k, bought for $5.0M.
   - 2022: Lanza, Żelewski and Fabčič, still the core today.
2. **Finished veterans.** A season or two before a title push, they add 3–4 players aged about 27–32 on **$230k–$295k** a week, with hall of famer or all-time great potential.
   - Examples: van der Zanden, Čorak, Kam Kiat Boon, Marinov, Montero Vargas, Dulęba, Mäkioja.
   - After the window they usually resell for 25–65% of the price.

**Weekly salary by age for their prospects ($k):**

| Age | 18 | 20 | 22 | 24 | 25 | 26 | 27 | 28 | 30 | 33 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lanza | 3.0 | 8.8 | 42 | 71 | 111 | 136 | 170 | 198 | 210 | 232 |
| Żelewski | 4.3 | 9.8 | 32 | 89 | 127 | 159 | 186 | 204 | 217 | 236 |
| Fabčič | 2.8 | 9.6 | 44 | 64 | 119 | 136 | 161 | 186 | 204 | 229 |
| Újszászi | 2.8 | 7.3 | 18 | 43 | 80 | 118 | 176 | 206 | 224 | — |

- Under $20k until age 21.
- A steep climb from 22 to 27.
- Flat from about 28.
- The titles came at about 29–33.

**Bench:** fillers on $4k–$15k who play 17–22 minutes a game. They are not trained; their salaries stay flat.

**Arena:** 40,550 seats, about 48–56% full. Estimated about $612k per game.
- **[Inferrence]** It earns through size, not efficiency, so it's not a good model to copy.

## 3. I Love Tehran: the same cycle, done earlier

**Results:**
- Bartar champions S37–41, including the B3 in S39–41.
- Relegated S42, then champions again S54–55 (B3 final in S54).
- Dormant since S57: payroll about $9k, world rank 14,736.

**Core payroll:**

| Stage | Seasons | Core payroll |
|---|---|---|
| Peak | S38–41 | $1.17M–$1.26M |
| Crash | S43–45 | about $60k |
| Peak again | S54–56 | about $1.21M–$1.24M |
| Collapse | S57 | $47k |

**Prospect batches:**
- **S31 (2014):** Cefarin, Birgersson and Hanlong, all about 20–21 on $7k–$10k, bought for $2.0M–$2.34M. They became the core of the titles and were resold for **$6.0M, $8.0M and $5.75M**.
- **S44 (2018):** Dlabač, Tajchert and Kovács, all about 18 on $3k–$4k, hall of famer potential, bought for $1.4M–$4.1M. They won the S54–55 titles at about 28–29 and were resold for $3.1M–$5.0M.

**Title veterans:** for S36–41, seven players aged about 29–33 on $100k–$236k, bought for **$5.0M–$6.7M** each.

**Salary curve:** the same shape as the Scorpions' (Dlabač: $3.7k at 18, $30k at 22, $118k at 24, $209k at 26, $247k at 28).

## 4. Młoty Stargard: always contending, funded by trading teenagers

**Results:**
- 29 Polish league titles, including 15 in a row (S35–49).
- Not relegated since S19, and in the B3 knockout rounds almost every season.

**Payroll stays flat:**
- Core payroll has been $650k–$1.22M every season since S30.
- Their best-paid player earned only **$124k–$190k** until S72 (now $244k).
- They carry 24–30 players a season.

**Core players:**
- Each season they add 1–2 players aged about 25–30, paying **$1.3M–$6.8M in transfer fees for players on only $42k–$173k a week**. Examples: Brochet ($5.67M / $48k), Homayounfar ($3.88M / $42k), Ðokaj ($6.84M / $83k).
- They keep them 5–10 seasons, then sell for 5–30% of the fee.
- **[Inferrence]** They pay a premium for players whose skills win games without pushing salary up.

**Money engine: the teenage flip.** Full list in `Mloty_Stargard_teen_flips.xlsx`.
- Since 2017 they bought 239 players aged 19 or under.
- **134 were resold at a profit**: $5.8M in, **$30.8M out, $25.1M profit**.
- **Potential:** 74 hall of famer, 49 MVP, 11 superstar. Players with allstar potential or lower were never resold; they were bought for $1k and later released.
- **Timing:** bought a median **7 days after the draft**, about half from the team that drafted them.
- **Prices:** median buy **$33k**, median sale **$198k** (6.8×), median **39 days** held. The salary never changes during the hold.
- **Playing time:** almost no competitive minutes (median 38 minutes in total).
- **Profile:** mostly PGs and SFs. Players over 210 cm sold highest. Nationalities are spread across 40 countries.
- **Sale price by potential:** median $229k for hall of famer, $191k for MVP, $130k for superstar.
- **Buying from the drafting team made no difference:** median sale $198k either way.
- **The engine is shrinking:** 20–27 flips a year in 2017–2020, 1–3 a year in 2024–2026.
- **[Inferrence]** Most of the gain probably comes from market timing (buying right after the draft, when few managers are bidding) and/or seeing the player's hidden skills after buying. It can't be proven from public data: training history is owner-only and scrimmage minutes aren't in team stats.

## 5. Młoty's flips: salaries against prices

Added 2026-09-24. Weekly salaries come from each player's public Salary History, matched to the season of purchase and sale using the official season dates (BB API). The figures are reproduced by `scripts/salary_vs_price.py`.

**At the time of the trade, every player was on a rookie wage.**
- Median salary in the season bought: **$3.6k a week** (range $2.1k–$8.0k).
- The salary stayed the same through the hold for 129 of 134 players.
- At the median, Młoty paid **8×** the weekly salary and sold for **59×** it. Half of the sales were between 44× and 78×.

**Młoty paid more for higher salaries; the resale market barely did.** A higher rookie salary means more skill already. Spearman correlation with salary: 0.47 for the buy price, 0.19 for the sale price.

| Salary in season bought | Players | Median buy | Median sale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under $3.0k | 31 | $22k | $187k |
| $3.0k–$3.6k | 35 | **$12k** | **$198k** |
| $3.6k–$4.0k | 17 | $22k | $206k |
| $4.0k–$5.0k | 32 | $47k | $198k |
| $5.0k and up | 19 | **$92k** | $250k |

- The widest margins came from draftees on $3.0k–$3.6k.
- The $5k+ group cost about 4× as much and sold for only about 25% more.

**Most of the players never became good.** 92 were bought in 2020 or earlier and are now at least 24. Salary is set by skill, so a player's highest salary shows roughly how good he became.
- Median highest salary: **$6.0k a week** (quartiles $3.6k / $6.0k / $12.9k).
- 12 ever reached $20k, 4 reached $50k, **none reached $100k**. The best was Salvátor Vírus, at $73k.
- By contrast, the Scorpions' prospects with the same potential went from $3k at 18 to $170k–$200k by 27.
- 36 of the 92 salary histories end before age 25. **[Inferrence]** Most were probably fired.

**A higher resale price pointed to a slightly better player:**

| Sale price (bought 2020 or earlier) | Median highest salary |
|---|---|
| Bottom third, $75k–$187k | $4.2k |
| Middle third, $191k–$275k | $5.1k |
| Top third, $275k–$600k | $9.8k |

- **[Inferrence]** The flip works because buyers pay for the potential label, and Młoty sells before anyone finds out how the player develops. The label is a real opportunity, as the Scorpions show. But most buyers didn't train these players for the 8–10 seasons it takes.
- Public data can't tell a weak prospect from one who was never trained. So "most never became good" may partly mean "most were never trained."

## 6. Comparison

| | Scorpions / I Love Tehran | Młoty Stargard |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Cycles: sell off → train prospects → 2–3 title seasons | Contends every season |
| Payroll | Swings between about $50k and $1.2M–$2.1M | Flat, about $0.7M–$1.2M |
| Core players | 18-year-old hall-of-famer prospects plus veterans on $230k–$295k | 25–30-year-olds on $42k–$173k, bought for high fees |
| Cash source | End-of-window sell-offs | Constant teenage flips |

## 7. What this means for Sharpshooters

- **Salary cap:** player pages now show a rule tied to world rank. At rank 13,701, Sharpshooters can only acquire players earning up to **$116,779** a week. The Scorpions' veterans on $230k–$295k are out of reach; many of Młoty's core players on $42k–$173k are not.
- **[Inferrence]** The teenage flip needs cash and market discipline, not world rank, since draftees earn $3k–$4k. Rules taken from Młoty's data:
  - Buy hall of famer potential first, MVP second, 18 years old.
  - Buy within about a week of the draft. Buying from the drafting team doesn't matter.
  - Prefer draftees on about $3.0k–$3.6k a week. They were the cheapest to buy and sold for as much as the others.
  - Pay up to about $50k–$80k, and stay near the low end for MVP potential.
  - Sell after 4–6 weeks, before the new season. Expect about $175k–$300k.
- **[Inferrence]** When buying a teenager to keep, don't treat the price as a sign of quality. On Młoty's data, a $300k 18-year-old most likely peaks around $10k a week. Visible skills and a long training plan matter far more.
- **[Inferrence]** The Scorpions' prospect route fits your training doctrine, but it takes about 10 seasons before the payoff.
- **Next step:** find which skills separate a $100k resale from a $300k one. The data would come from transfer-list draftees, where skills are visible, run through the salary-model workflow.

## Files

- `Mloty_Stargard_teen_flips.xlsx`: 134 flips with name, ID link, nationality, age, potential, position, height, draft pick, prices, dates, profit and days held. Also salary in the seasons bought and sold, price ÷ salary, salary at 22/24/26, highest salary and last season. Plus a Summary sheet.
- `mloty_stargard_teen_flips.csv`: the same player table as plain CSV.
- `data/salary_history.json`: each player's weekly salary by season.
- `data/seasons.csv`: official season start and finish dates.
- `scripts/`: the scripts that fetch the data, add the salary columns and print section 5's figures.

## Caveats

- Ages marked "estimated" are 17 + (season bought − draft season). Checked against active players, they're exact or one year low.
- "Salary when bought" is the player's Salary History value for the season of purchase.
- Payroll figures count every player who played in a season, with their salary for that season, so mid-season arrivals and departures are counted fully.
- Finances, skills and training settings of other teams are private. Anything about them is inferred.
